# Tatsuya Okuda

**導入を決めるのは経営者、毎日使うのは現場の人。現場に「入ってよかった」と思われるシステムを、無理のないコストで作る。**

メガバンク系SIerのエンジニアとしてキャリアを始め、デザイン、教育、事業運営まで自分の手で経験してきました。課題の聞き取りから設計・実装・運用、導入後の定着、そして新規事業開発まで。いまは、取引先の業務システムの開発を続けながら、そこで作ったツールを自社サービスとして展開する準備を進め、並行して自分のプロダクトの保守と新機能の開発を行っています。

## Highlights

- **1件のLINE bot案件から、6つの業務システムへ** — 採用・人材サービス企業の業務を、ディレクター兼エンジニアとして現場ごとにシステム化
- **1つの適性検査を3社へ、1つのAI応答基盤を4つの現場へ** — 個別の開発で終わらせず、展開できる形に設計
- **非エンジニアが自分で中身を確認できる形に** — 設定やデータを現場が触れる場所に置き、担当者が自分で見て直せる状態で引き渡す
- **自社プロダクト** — ソクトケ（行政書士試験の対策アプリ／Web・iOSで公開、Android版を開発中）、Gosai（iOSアプリ）

## How I work

- 導入を決めるのは経営層でも、実際に使うのは現場の社員です。両方から話を聞いてから設計します
- AIが出てきて、たいていのものは作れるようになりました。だからこそ、作れることと必要なことは別だと考えています。「これがかっこいい」「便利そう」と自分が思う機能を足すのではなく、相手が本当に必要としているものだけを残します
- 足すより、減らすほうが効く場面は多いです。機能を増やす前に、やめられる作業がないかを先に考えます
- 何を作るか・作らないかの判断は、現場が使い続けているかどうかを、１つの基準にしています

Executives decide which systems to buy; the people who use them every day are the staff on the floor. I build business tools that the frontline is actually glad to have, at a cost the business can carry.

AI has made it easy to build almost anything, which makes restraint the real skill. Engineers often ship what they personally find elegant. I would rather listen first, remove what nobody needs, and keep only what earns its place.

Engineering. My work with a recruiting-services company began with a single LINE bot. Instead of handing it over and leaving, I watched how the staff used it, found the next problem, proposed a fix, and built again. That loop has grown into six business systems — an aptitude-test product now licensed to three companies, a response engine running across four workplaces, an AI interview service in development. My title is engineer; the actual job is closing the gap between what management wants and what the frontline can use.

Running businesses. From 2022 to 2026 I co-founded and ran a video-editing school. We did everything ourselves: ad campaigns, social media, course design, coaching students who were about to give up, and closing the books at month end. A business has seasons for growing and seasons for holding; those four years taught me that protecting what works, rather than expanding at any cost, is often what makes the next step possible — and that a business has to be read in numbers: what a customer costs, what keeps people going, what is actually left at the end. Since 2025 I have also run a company in Georgia that helps Japanese clients open bank accounts and set up companies there: reading bank and registry requirements, preparing documents, and interpreting in meetings across languages and time zones.

Design, teaching, coaching.
